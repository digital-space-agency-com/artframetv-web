Choosing an art screensaver for Android TV: what to look for

There are several ways to get art on an idle Android TV, from Google's own Ambient Mode to third-party apps. Rather than rank them, here is a checklist that tells you which one suits your TV and your taste, plus where Art Frame TV fits.

1. Can it be your actual screensaver?

On Google TV, Ambient Mode only lists Google's sources, so a third-party app needs a launcher override or an ADB command to become the screensaver. Check that the app tells you how. If it also works when opened from its icon, you lose nothing by trying it first. See how to change the screensaver on Google TV.

2. Where does the art come from?

Look for public-domain or properly licensed images. Art from museum open-access collections is free to show on your wall. If an app does not say where its images come from, assume you cannot use them commercially, and ask.

3. Does it frame the picture?

Many screensavers show the image full screen. That crops the picture or leaves black bars. A mat and frame makes the TV read as a print. Art Frame TV's whole purpose is this, with a bevelled mat, frame mouldings and a depth shadow.

4. How does it charge?

Prefer one-time purchases over subscriptions for something you will leave on a wall. Check what the free version includes, and whether it has a trial that quietly expires. Art Frame TV's free version has no time limit, and its paid options are one-time.

5. What does it do with your data?

A screensaver has no reason to need an account. Check the privacy policy, and whether your own photos are uploaded anywhere. Art Frame TV has no account system and keeps photos on the TV.

6. Will it look good on your panel?

Whatever you choose, lower the backlight to 25 to 35% and use a warm picture mode. See how to turn your Android TV into art for the settings, and the Samsung Frame alternatives page for how a normal TV compares with a Frame.

Common questions

What is the best art screensaver for Android TV?
It depends on what you value. Choose one that can run as the screensaver, uses properly licensed art, frames the picture, has fair pricing and respects your privacy. Art Frame TV is built around those points.
Does Google TV have a built-in art screensaver?
Yes. Ambient Mode has an Art gallery source with Google's own curated images. It does not add frames or mats, and it does not accept third-party sources.
Are art screensavers safe for OLED TVs?
Static images are the risk. Choose an app that changes the picture regularly or drifts it slightly, and keep the backlight low.
Should I pay for a screensaver app?
Only if the free version proves it is worth it. A one-time purchase for extra frames or art is fairer than a subscription for a wall display.
